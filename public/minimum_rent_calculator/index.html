How this works

Add up what the whole building costs each month: financing costs (principal and interest, taxes, insurance, mortgage insurance, any other loans for the building), utilities you pay, repairs, savings for big replacements, pay for your time, and an emergency and planning fund. The rented units cover their share of that total. In a 2-flat where you live in one unit, the rented unit covers half. In a 3-flat where you live in one, the two rented units cover two thirds between them. The unit you live in carries the rest, as your own housing cost. Savings for vacancies come from how often you get a new tenant and how long it takes to re-rent: one month empty every two years means a unit earns rent 23 of 24 months, so rent is set to cover a full year of costs in that time.

The interest-only view counts just the interest part of the mortgage, based on what you still owe today. The principal part is money you keep, as equity in the building.

Address lookups estimate taxes as assessed value x state equalizer, minus the homeowner exemption if you live there ($10,000, plus $8,000 more if you are 65 or older), x the tax rate. The mortgage estimate assumes 20% down and the average 30-year rate in the year of the last sale. Your real numbers are better if you have them.

Educational tool, not financial, legal, or tax advice. Estimates only. Data: Cook County Open Data (Assessor) and Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey. Chicago RLTO rules are not modeled.
