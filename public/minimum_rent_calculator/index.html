How this works

Add up what the whole building costs each month: mortgage, any other loans for the building, taxes, insurance, utilities you pay, repairs, savings for big replacements, pay for your time, and savings funds. The rented units cover their share of that total. In a 2-flat where you live in one unit, the rented unit covers half. In a 3-flat where you live in one, the two rented units cover two thirds between them. The unit you live in carries the rest, as your own housing cost. A cushion for vacancy and unpaid rent is added on top.

The interest-only view counts just the interest part of the mortgage, based on what you still owe today. The principal part is money you keep, as equity in the building.

Address lookups estimate taxes as assessed value x state equalizer, minus the homeowner exemption if you live there ($10,000, plus $8,000 more if you are 65 or older), x the tax rate. The mortgage estimate assumes 20% down and the average 30-year rate in the year of the last sale. Your real numbers are better if you have them.

Educational tool, not financial, legal, or tax advice. Estimates only. Data: Cook County Open Data (Assessor) and Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey. Chicago RLTO rules are not modeled. Nothing you enter leaves your browser; only an address lookup is sent to Cook County. No tracking, accounts, or storage.
