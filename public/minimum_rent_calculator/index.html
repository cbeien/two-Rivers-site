How this works

Add up what it costs to run the building each month: mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities you pay, repairs, savings for big replacements (cost divided by years it lasts), pay for your time, and reserves for emergencies. The rented units cover their share of that total: in a 2-flat where you live in one unit, the rented unit covers half; in a 3-flat where you live in one, the two rented units cover two thirds between them. The unit you live in carries the rest, as your own housing cost. A cushion for vacancy and unpaid rent is added on top as a percentage of rent.

The interest-only view counts just the interest part of the mortgage payment, based on what you still owe today. The principal part is money you keep, as equity in the building.

Address lookups estimate taxes as assessed value x state equalizer, minus the homeowner exemption if you live there ($10,000, plus $8,000 more if you are 65 or older), x the tax rate. Your real bill is better if you have it.

Educational tool, not financial, legal, or tax advice. Estimates only. Data: Cook County Open Data (Assessor) and Freddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey (annual averages of the 30-year fixed rate). Chicago RLTO rules are not modeled.
